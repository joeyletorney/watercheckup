## Spoken script

I’m Joe Letorney. I’ve spent 30 years in water treatment, and here’s where I’d start before buying a filter.

First, what are you trying to change? Better taste, a concern about lead, and hard-water scale are different problems. The same product won’t necessarily address all three.

Second, what fits your home? A pitcher needs refilling but no plumbing. A countertop reverse-osmosis unit needs space, power and tank refills. An under-sink system needs an installation. And a whole-house carbon filter isn’t the same thing as a water softener.

Third, what will it take to maintain it? Check replacement-filter costs, the rated capacity and the replacement schedule—not just the purchase price.

Then check the exact model’s documented reduction claim for your concern. A certification number alone doesn’t mean it covers everything.
